## Two entry paths, and only one of them asks the share question

**Path A — a bank is connected.** We can see money the user never chose to show us, so we must ask
what is in scope. Accept either form:

```
consideredBalance = min(stated, Σ non-excluded balances − ring-fenced)   -- ABSOLUTE, "$50,000"
consideredBalance = (Σ non-excluded balances − ring-fenced) × pct        -- PERCENTAGE, "60%"
```

The `min` matters: if the user names a figure **above** their real balance, clamp to the balance and
say so, rather than planning against money that is not there.

**Path B — manual entry, no bank.** The user has already chosen what is in scope by choosing what to
type. **Asking for a percentage here is incoherent** — take the figure whole. If they later want more
in scope they raise the total, and the app does not need to know where it came from and should not
ask.

Both paths produce the same `ConsideredFunds`, from two sources that both exist on day one:

```
ConsideredFundsSource
├── BankConsideredFunds    → balance, minus exclusions, times pct or clamped to a stated figure
└── ManualConsideredFunds  → the stated total, taken whole
```

## Ring-fencing

Three routes to one "set aside" total, and it is shown **explicitly**:

- an **excluded account** — invisible to every calculation;
- an **excluded transaction** — the $7,000-from-family case, flagged, never reaching income or
  surplus;
- the **withheld percentage** from Path A.

Money the user cannot see is money they stop trusting the app about, so never hide it silently.

## Tax, and the trap

Payroll deposits are **already net of tax**. Applying a rate on top quietly removes another 20% of
money the user actually has. So one boolean at onboarding decides everything:

- **"My income arrives already taxed"** (employed — the common case) → **no tax line at all**. The
  resolved rate is used only to explain the gross-to-net gap if asked.
- **"My income arrives untaxed"** (freelance, common in Lebanon) → a `TAX_RESERVE` category funded at
  the resolved rate, `LOCKED` by default, because a tax reserve is not something to offer to cut.

The rate resolves through the **same layer chain as baselines** — the user's own figure wins and stays
private to that account, while everyone else keeps the seeded country rate. Store it as an **effective
rate labelled `ESTIMATED`**: real systems are progressive with brackets and allowances, and a single
percentage is an approximation, not a statutory truth.

## The discretionary floor

The floor is the minimum spending on life that the engine is **not allowed to touch**. Without one it
defaults to zero, and then a short goal makes the tradeoff engine say *"cut Entertainment to $0, cut
Dining Out to $0"* — arithmetically correct and advice nobody follows.

**Always ask the user for it — and explain what you are asking.** "What is your discretionary floor?"
is not a question anyone can answer. Use `SpendingQuestion.monthlyFloor(...)`, which is already in
`profile/domain` for this:

> **What is the least you would want to spend each month on enjoying life?**
> We will never suggest cutting below this, even to reach a goal faster.
>
> - Going out and fun — nights out, cinema, games, sports, hobbies
> - Eating out — restaurants, cafes, takeaway, snacks and coffee
> - Clothes — clothing, shoes and personal items
>
> *Suggested: $216 — typical for someone in Beirut who goes out regularly*

Three things that make it answerable, and all three are required: **what it covers**, in the user's
words with examples; **why it is being asked**, so the number feels like protection rather than
interrogation; and **what the suggestion is based on**, since a pre-filled figure with no stated
grounding reads as arbitrary.

`SpendingQuestion.explainedCoverage()` builds those bullets from `SpendCategory.label()` and
`covers()`, so adding a category updates the explanation automatically. **Do not hand-write that list
in a template** — it would drift the first time the taxonomy changes.

**Never show an internal term in the interface.** Not "discretionary", not "floor", not "baseline",
not "rigidity", and never a constant name like `DINING_OUT`. Every category already carries a
`label()` for exactly this.

The default exists only for when they decline, skip onboarding, or arrive without a lifestyle tier —
it is a fallback, never the primary path.

### Deriving the default

Grounded in BLS CEX 2024, where total annual expenditure is $78,535: entertainment is 4.6%, food away
from home $3,945 (5.0%), apparel and services 2.5%. **Typical discretionary spending is therefore
about 12% of expenditure.** Households spend close to their net income, so treating that 12% as a
share of net income is a reasonable approximation — state it in the README rather than hiding it.

The floor is a *fraction* of that typical figure, because if the floor equalled typical spending no
cut would ever be possible:

```
floor = typicalDiscretionary × tierFraction × obligationMultiplier
```

| Lifestyle tier | Fraction of typical | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `HOMEBODY` | 0.25 | Most life happens at home; little to protect |
| `OCCASIONAL` | 0.35 | |
| `REGULAR` | 0.45 | |
| `FREQUENT` | 0.55 | Going out *is* their life; cutting it is the change they will refuse |

Then adjust for how loaded they already are, since someone with little left after rent and loans
cannot also protect a large lifestyle budget:

| `fixedCommitments / netIncome` | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| ≤ 0.40 — comfortable | 1.15 |
| 0.40 – 0.55 | 1.00 |
| 0.55 – 0.70 | 0.85 |
| > 0.70 — stretched | 0.70 |

**Clamp the result to 3%–12% of net income.** The lower bound stops a heavily indebted user being
told to live on nothing; the upper stops the floor swallowing the whole surplus and making every goal
look infeasible.

Worked check: `REGULAR`, obligations at 45% of income, $4,000/mo net → 12% × 0.45 × 1.00 ≈ 5.4% ≈
**$216/mo** protected across entertainment, dining and clothing combined. Roughly $50 a week, which
is a credible minimum rather than a generous one.

### Where it lives

A table, never constants in code, so changing policy is editing rows:

```sql
lifestyle_floor(tier, category, pct_of_baseline)
```

Expressing it as a percentage **of that category's local baseline** rather than as dollars means it
scales with the city automatically — the same row works for Beirut and San Francisco, and adding a
country needs no new code.

## Hard constraint

`ConsideredFunds` reaches the plan **only** by raising a goal's `saved` side — which shrinks
`requiredMonthly` — and by producing a runway figure (`consideredBalance / monthlyDeficit`) for
display. **It must never be passed into the allocator.** The allocator takes a single `Money` surplus
and nothing else; that seam is the most valuable structural property in the project, and
`ArchitectureTest` guards it.

**Write the tests the specs are missing** for the floor, since it postdates them. At minimum: the

same income; a heavily obligated user's floor is reduced but never below the 3% clamp; the floor never
exceeds the 12% clamp; and a user-entered figure overrides the computed default entirely.
