## What this is

The goal engine at a shorter horizon, and **it needs no AI**. The allocator answers *"can I reach
$3,000 by March?"*; this answers *"can I afford $25 today?"* Both take a budget, subtract what is
committed, spread the remainder over the periods left, and compare.

Define `SpendDecision` over a **category**, not over meals — it is the identical function for a $200
jacket, and generalising costs nothing because the category is already a parameter. Meals are simply
the first surface.

```
sustainableDaily = (allowance − spentMonthToDate) / remainingDays
verdict          = COMFORTABLE | SUSTAINABLE | OVER_BUDGET
catchUpPerDay    = sustainableDaily − (remaining − ticket) / (remainingDays − 1)
```

**Pure and clock-free**, like the allocator: the date is a parameter. `ArchitectureTest` enforces it.
`Money.spreadOver` already rounds up, which is the safe direction here — a catch-up that cuts slightly
more than strictly needed errs toward the user staying on track.

## Rules the behaviour must follow

- **The verdict always carries the next cheaper band and its own verdict.** A warning alone is a
  scold; the point is to say what *would* work.
- **Band prices are lookup data scaled by the city** — `meal_band(band, multiplier_of_typical_meal)`
  against that city's `DINING_OUT` baseline — so Beirut and San Francisco work from one table rather
  than two branches. Adding a band is one row.
- **Observed prices replace estimates once data exists.** `merchant_entity_id` from P3 is stable, so
  the real average ticket is a `GROUP BY`, not a model. An LLM asked the same question returns a
  confident, unverifiable number feeding the same arithmetic — the failure mode already rejected for
  cost-of-living data.
- **Rebalancing respects rigidity absolutely.** Raising one item by ΔX takes ΔX from the others in
  rigidity order — `DISPOSABLE` first, `ESSENTIAL` last, **`LOCKED` never**, neither cut nor raised.
  Nothing goes below its floor, which is its localBaseline.
- **A `LOCKED` item yields a `SavingHint`**, qualitative and never entering any total: rent gets
  *"a roommate or a lease renegotiation is the only real lever here"*, a locked gym membership gets
  *"off-peak or annual-paid memberships are usually 20–30% cheaper"*. Hints are lookup data, one row
  each. Keeping them non-numeric is what stops a tip silently becoming a number in the plan.
- **When an increase cannot be absorbed, report the two real options** — raise the considered share of
  the balance, or accept a goal slipping — rather than silently producing an impossible budget.
